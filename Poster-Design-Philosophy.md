# Court Light

**A design movement of athletic clarity.**

Court Light is the aesthetics of the marked ground: the painted rectangle, the
service line, the kitchen. Every court is a diagram an athlete trusts with their
body — lines laid down once, precisely, and obeyed for decades. This movement
borrows that trust. Composition is court-marking: a small number of straight,
confident divisions of the plane, each placed as if a mis-struck line meant
repainting the whole floor. Nothing decorative survives; whatever remains is
load-bearing. The result must read as the work of a master sign-painter who
measured twice and struck once — meticulously crafted, the product of deep
expertise and painstaking attention.

Space behaves like a court at dawn: a deep, saturated green ground, generous
and calm, interrupted only where play demands it. Margins are wide and
inviolable — the out-of-bounds that gives the game its shape. Elements never
crowd; they take position. Rhythm comes from repetition with athletic
discipline: rows of identical marks, evenly drilled, like footwork practice.
Dense accumulations of small, perfect elements (perforations, dots, hatch
lines) reward close viewing and prove patient labor.

Color is a strict three-voice chord. The ground: forest green, dark enough to
make white lines glow. The signal: chartreuse-lime, the exact optical shout of
the perforated ball — used sparingly, always for what matters most, never for
mood. The structure: white, thin and exact, with one cool blue undertone
allowed as a shadow voice. No gradients that smear; only clean planes and
crisp edges, calibrated like paint mixed to a federation standard.

Typography is scoreboard and stencil: tall, tight, unapologetic for the few
words that deserve size; hairline and letter-spaced for everything supporting.
Text is a visual material, kerned and aligned to the court grid — a number can
be monumental, a label must whisper. Words never explain; they anchor. Every
baseline sits on the same invisible net-cord of the layout.

The finished artifact should feel like an official document from an imaginary
athletics federation: systematic, numbered, reverent about something as simple
as a game — and unmistakably labored over. Alignments exact to the millimeter,
optical corrections made by eye, spacing tuned through countless refinements.
Master-level execution or nothing: the viewer should sense that whoever made
this could not sleep until the lines were true.
